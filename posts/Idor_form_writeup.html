IDOR in Form Configuration Endpoint Leading to Unauthorized Form Data Access and Persistent Access After Removal

Overview: While hunting on examble.com, I was collecting JS files, endpoints, old files, and directory paths during recon. I came across an interesting endpoint: GET /api/form/{formId}/PC. After creating an account, I found no forms feature on the main app, which led me to discover forms.examble.com via subdomain enumeration. By combining a group member's ability to view form IDs through the UI with the unauthenticated /PC endpoint, a removed or resigned group member retains persistent read access to all form configurations they previously observed — bypassing access revocation entirely.

01  RECON

Discovery & Analysis

While hunting on examble.com, I was collecting JS files served by the application and analyzing them for interesting endpoints — alongside old files, exposed directories, and other recon artifacts.

Inside one of the collected files, I found an endpoint that immediately caught my attention:

GET /api/form/{formId}/PC

The naming suggested it was fetching form configuration data by ID. Since I hadn't interacted with the application yet, I created an account and logged in — but found no forms-related feature anywhere on the main app.

This prompted me to enumerate subdomains, where I discovered:

$ forms.examble.com

I created an account on this subdomain and found a full-featured form builder. Upon creating a form, the application assigned it a UUID-based ID — visible directly in the UI.

02  EXPLOIT

Step 1 — Unauthenticated Form Data Access

I created a test form, grabbed its UUID, and sent a request to the /PC endpoint with no session or authorization header:

$ curl -i "https://forms.examble.com/api/form/4fc69355-dab0-4892-8777-bcdcba7f3836/PC"

The server returned the full form configuration — completely unauthenticated:

{ "name": "alpha form-test", "properties": { "notifySubscribers": true, "css": null, "javaScript": null, "theme": { "themeId": "default", "primary": "#008EFF" }, "authentication": 0, ... }, "license": 10, "region": "eu" }

At this point, the impact seemed limited — the form ID is a UUID and not something an attacker could realistically guess on its own. So I looked for a way to obtain valid IDs without guessing.

Step 2 — Chaining with Group Membership to Harvest IDs

I explored the application further and discovered a groups system, where an owner can invite other members to collaborate on forms within a shared workspace.

I added a second account to a group as a regular member. As a member, clicking on any form reveals its UUID directly in the browser's URL bar — making it trivial to collect all form IDs shared within the group.

  • ✅  Group member can view all form UUIDs via the URL bar
  • ✅  No special privilege required — standard member access is enough
  • ✅  IDs remain valid even after leaving or being removed from the group

Step 3 — Persistent Access After Removal

The critical issue: even after being removed from the group (or voluntarily leaving), the previously collected form IDs remain fully valid against the unauthenticated /PC endpoint. The application has no mechanism to invalidate or scope access to these IDs after membership is revoked.

# After being removed from the group... curl -i "https://forms.examble.com/api/form/4fc69355-dab0-4892-8777-bcdcba7f3836/PC" # Still returns full form configuration ✅

A removed member retains full read access to every form they observed during their membership — indefinitely — with zero re-authentication required.

03  CONCLUSION

Since the form ID alone is sufficient to retrieve full form configuration data without authentication, and given that any group member can trivially collect form IDs during their membership by observing the browser URL, a removed or resigned member retains persistent, unauthorized read access to all forms they previously had legitimate access to — completely bypassing the group's access revocation mechanism and any role-based controls the application intends to enforce.
